## 0.8 Arsenal — current release contract

This section supersedes the progression, barrier, debris and audio portions below. The user asked for removal of added wall-obstacles, a complete sound overhaul, actual boss weapon/personal upgrades, substantially higher difficulty and large boss health pools. Delivery remains the existing private GitHub repository and main merge, without deploying another host. Title version must follow package.json.

David's 13 November decision takes the shield out of the game (MASTER-PROMPT §6 ter point 1). No shield energy, no blocked shot, no shield bash, no defensive wave depending on it, no pickup, no equipment piece, no HUD gauge and no audio cue. `input.fireLeft` is now only the left-hand trigger, with no second role. What remains defensive is the dash's short invulnerability window, the `input.melee` gun-butt strike and the `input.pulse` wave, which is independent of any equipment. Damage that the guard used to absorb now lands normally, and the world's opening guard pickup became `arsenal-smg-cells` (24 submachine-gun cartridges at the same position). Checkpoints written before this decision cannot be restored, because the removal changed `progression` and that pickup and therefore the world fingerprint; the refusal is clean (no throw, no partial mutation) and the shell falls back to sector-entry equipment. Where a section below still describes a guard, this paragraph governs.

Fresh runs explicitly call `FWorld.create(seed,index,{campaign:true,campaignVersion:2})`. The first6/18 worlds equip the player, the last12 use the full arsenal. Start pistol4 right, left hand empty. Boss1 gives shotgun1; boss2 SMG0; boss3 grenade3; boss4 repeats grenade3 as a calibration/ammo reward as communicated to the user; boss5 provides upgrades; boss6 gives rocket2. All18 bosses additionally grant a weapon-specific calibration and rotating personal upgrade when their physical crate is collected. No free trophy or shop may bypass the order. The crate's remaining resources and one-time principal reward are independent; the exit requires collection of the principal reward.

The economy v2 retains the existing five personal keys with max12 and adds five bounded weapon ranks with max8. `FEconomy.weaponStats` is the authority for actual attack calibration, including explosions. Passive armor cannot reduce incoming damage below one half by itself. Increased maximum health/capacity is not a refill. A portable campaign v2 profile carries the exact discovered set and sorted unique `claimedBossRewards`. Snapshot restoration validates ranks, inventory, ledger and crate state together before any mutation; a replay or partly full inventory cannot duplicate upgrades. Old campaign/economy versions continue through their original world variation.

New world boss health runs from600 to12000, with constant entry health during inventory changes. Engagement caps6–16, stronger damage/cadence and larger finite rosters increase difficulty. Announced areas, core exposure, counterplay and bounded active entities remain. Controlled DPS/ammo checks must be distinguished from a human playthrough.

For v2, `reinforcement_baffle`, `reinforcement_frame`, `reinforcement_mark` and their collision boxes are absent. Natural candidate entries use existing walkable corners, neighbor rooms and corridor turns. Runtime still enforces distance, complete silhouette occlusion, physical clearance and a navigable path; no visible spawn is permitted merely because the camera faces away. Reserve homes remain in their original rooms. `naturalEntries` encounters derive their leash from the candidate positions; an arriving actor follows the actual nav route to the encounter anchor, never an unseen player coordinate. Bounded `entryGrace` (0–60 seconds) prevents premature return/sleep during that approach and counts even distant arriving actors toward the combat cap. Optional v2-only `reinforcementVersion:1` snapshots preserve and validate the timer; older versions do not acquire the field. Classic/v1 geometry fingerprints remain exact.

Destruction derives structural pieces from removed geometry, retaining primitive, dimensions, rotation and material. Decorative cuts preserve untouched pieces instead of deleting an entire group. Contact causes bounce/friction/settlement, not expiry; destroyed door leaves also produce plates. Persistent pieces are bounded128, temporary chips64; nearest structural pieces have rendering priority under the existing backend caps. Rubble is cosmetic to player/navigation and does not simulate chunk-to-chunk stacks. Optional rubbleVersion1 serializes and validates poses/timers/IDs atomically; older checkpoints lacking rubble remain valid.

Audio uses an original deterministic PCM bank prepared before combat. Weapon reports, footsteps, impacts, breaks and landings are material- and energy-aware. No continuous pitched drone or synthetic bird cues remain. Outdoor wind includes gaps; motors and steam require nearby live sources and route to ambience. `FSoundscape` uses3D distance, yaw panning, intact-wall/closed-door occlusion and at most12 ray probes per125ms, with bounded caches invalidated by world/destruction/doors. Menu pause and explicit test playback preserve mute and bus settings. The three MOD compositions remain unchanged. PCM metrics do not imply subjective listening or device testing.

The new save envelope stays version3 and adds campaignRevision2; internal campaignVersion2/economyVersion2 discriminate the new profile. Cache keys include campaign variation, so resuming v1 and starting v2 cannot reuse the wrong world. Historical classic version2 envelopes still resume unchanged. The same dynamic title version badge is preserved. Source, tests, distribution, measured SIZE.json, screenshots and validation evidence must agree at release.

Host duties for the event queue and the growing state arrays (engine work lot 02 T17, SIM-13). `sim.state.events` is a queue owned by the host. The simulation appends to it during `sim.step()` and during the direct calls that can emit, such as `sim.buy()` (`purchase`); it never reads, trims or caps it, and `restore()` replaces it with a new empty array. After every `sim.step()` and every such call, the host must consume the events and then empty the array in place (`sim.state.events.length=0`), as `events()` in `src/game.js` does after each fixed substep and after a purchase. The host reads `sim.state.events` again after `restore()` instead of keeping a reference to the old array. Emptying is a memory duty only, since the simulation never reads the queue: over 10,000 steps on two worlds, the state digests and the final snapshot are identical with and without it. A host that never empties it keeps every event of the session (800 to 1,212 per 10,000 steps with the proof pilot). Tests and benches that keep the events of a bounded run stay valid. The simulation bounds the other arrays itself: `particles` at most 180, `explosions` 24, `projectiles` 48, `bullets` 160, `debris` 128 structural pieces (`MAX_RUBBLE`) plus 64 chips (`MAX_CHIPS`); `bossAttacks` expire after their duration, and restore accepts at most 16. `loot` holds one entry per killed enemy that carries a reward, taken entries included, so its length never exceeds `world.enemies.length`: 120/140/170 in the classic sectors, 42 to 170 in campaign v1 and 66 to 221 in campaign v2, identical for the five seeds checked. Restore enforces the same bound. Taken entries must stay, because restore requires an entry for each rewarded dead enemy: compacting them changes the checkpoint content and belongs to a save-format revision (engine work lot 12). Observed maxima over 10,000 steps of the proof pilot on six worlds, with the player's health topped up: 143 debris, 50 particles, 2 projectiles, 3 explosions, 12 bullets, 18 events in one step and 5 loot entries, one per kill. Evidence: `docs/chantier-moteur/preuves/lot-02/t17/`.
